# Duke Tech Expo 2026 — Panel Discussion
## Revised Brief: "AI Automation, Worker Transition, and Company-Level Action"

---

## NARRATIVE FRAME

**"National reform is slow. But companies can act NOW. Here's what that looks like and why it's actually good business."**

### Key Premise
- AI automation of jobs is inevitable and often efficient — don't fight it
- The real problem: When jobs disappear, worker income disappears, but corporations still profit
- This is **unsustainable** for both workers and companies long-term (no income = no consumers = no market)
- **Solution:** Companies can invest in worker transition TODAY, even without national policy changes

### Why Companies Should Care (Business Case)
1. **Turnover costs money** — Recruiting + training a new hire costs ~1.5-3x annual salary. Investing in retraining existing workers is often cheaper
2. **Community stability = business continuity** — Communities where companies invest in transition have healthier talent pools, better consumer spending, lower crime
3. **Long-term profitability** — Companies investing in worker welfare show better retention, higher productivity, lower risk of labor strife
4. **Corporate reputation** — Post-2026, stakeholders (investors, customers, employees) increasingly reward companies that handle automation ethically

## THE GRAPHS (DUAL FRAMING)

### PART A: THE MACRO PROBLEM (Sets Urgency)

**Why these graphs matter:** They establish that automation is real, income is falling, and someone needs to act.

#### Graph 1: Automation Pace vs. Job Loss (By Sector, 2020-2030)
- **What it shows:** Which industries are automating fastest + where jobs are disappearing
- **Why:** Shows automation isn't hypothetical — it's happening NOW in specific sectors
- **Audience takeaway:** "This is your industry. What's your company doing?"
- **Data sources:** BLS, Anthropic labor market research, WEF Future of Jobs 2026
- **Example:** Manufacturing, customer service, data entry showing steepest job loss

#### Graph 2: Worker Income vs. Automation Intensity (By Company/Sector)
- **What it shows:** As companies automate more, what happens to average worker wages?
- **Why:** Proves the core problem — automation = profit for companies, job loss for workers
- **Audience takeaway:** "Corporations are capturing the gains. Workers are paying the price."
- **Data sources:** BLS wage data + company earnings reports + automation adoption metrics
- **Example:** Tech sector: productivity up 35%, median worker wage up 3%

#### Graph 3: Cost of Living vs. Median Income (By Region, 2026)
- **What it shows:** How many workers are living paycheck-to-paycheck even WITH employment?
- **Why:** Shows that even "employed" people are vulnerable; automation = immediate crisis
- **Audience takeaway:** "These aren't abstract statistics. People can't afford rent."
- **Data sources:** Census Bureau, Bureau of Labor Statistics, MIT Living Wage Calculator
- **Example:** NC median income $55K, cost of living $60K (deficit before automation)

#### Graph 4: Automation Speed vs. Retraining Capacity (Timeline)
- **What it shows:** How fast are jobs being automated vs. how fast can people retrain?
- **Why:** Proves that "just learn to code" is mathematically impossible at scale
- **Audience takeaway:** "The gap isn't closeable via individual effort. Structural solutions needed."
- **Data sources:** WEF skills gap data, community college enrollment capacity, reskilling timelines
- **Example:** 59% of workforce needs reskilling by 2030 (2B people), but retraining takes 1-3 years

#### Graph 5: Consumer Spending Decline in Automation-Hit Communities
- **What it shows:** Communities where large employers automated without transition support saw retail spending drop, economic decline
- **Why:** Proves the business case — if workers have no income, markets collapse
- **Audience takeaway:** "Automation without transition kills the customer base."
- **Data sources:** Census economic data, retail sales data, regional economic indicators
- **Example:** Rust Belt cities vs. communities with proactive transition programs

### PART B: THE COMPANY-LEVEL SOLUTIONS (Actionable)

**Why these graphs matter:** They show what companies CAN do, the math behind why they should, and proof it works.

#### Graph 6: Turnover Cost vs. Transition Investment (ROI Analysis)
- **What it shows:** Cost to replace a worker (recruiting, training, productivity loss) vs. cost to retrain them for new role
- **Why:** Shows that transition investment is often CHEAPER than turnover
- **Audience takeaway:** "Helping workers transition is good business, not charity."
- **Data sources:** HR metrics, Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) data, company case studies
- **Example:** Replacing mid-level worker costs ~$100K; retraining costs ~$25-40K

#### Graph 7: Worker Retention by Transition Investment (Comparison)
- **What it shows:** Companies that invest heavily in worker transition vs. those that don't (retention rates, tenure, productivity)
- **Why:** Proves that workers who feel supported stay longer and perform better
- **Audience takeaway:** "Transition programs keep good people."
- **Data sources:** Company HR data, industry benchmarks, academic studies on retention
- **Example:** Companies with formal transition programs: 85% retention vs. 65% without

#### Graph 8: Long-Term Profitability: "Fair Transition" vs. "Cheap Automation"
- **What it shows:** 10-year profitability comparison: companies that invested in worker transition vs. those that didn't
- **Why:** Tests the hypothesis that "ethical automation" is actually MORE profitable long-term
- **Audience takeaway:** "This isn't sacrifice. It's smarter business."
- **Data sources:** Company earnings reports, Socially Responsible Investment (SRI) metrics, long-term stock performance
- **Example:** Companies with worker-friendly automation policies outperformed peers 2020-2026

#### Graph 9: Community Health Where Companies Invested vs. Abandoned
- **What it shows:** Economic and social metrics (income, homeownership, crime, health) in communities with transition support vs. those without
- **Why:** Proves that company investment in workers = community stability = long-term customer base
- **Audience takeaway:** "Investing in workers is investing in your market."
- **Data sources:** Census data, local economic data, community health metrics
- **Example:** Auto industry cities with transition programs vs. those without

#### Graph 10: Wage-Sharing & Profit-Sharing Models (Company Examples)
- **What it shows:** Different company models: profit-sharing, automation-gain-sharing, wage preservation, UBI-style programs
- **Why:** Shows concrete options companies can choose TODAY
- **Audience takeaway:** "Here are the levers. Pick one."
- **Data sources:** Company annual reports, employee surveys, innovation research
- **Example:** 
  - Patagonia: profit-sharing (35% of profits to workers + community)
  - Salesforce: $100M equality fund (closing wage gaps)
  - Scandinavia: mandatory profit-sharing laws (workers get piece of automation gains)

## COMPANY-LEVEL ACTION IDEAS (For Discussion)

### What DCRI + Pfizer Could Do Together

#### Model 1: Co-Funded Transition Program
- **Setup:** When Pfizer automates roles, DCRI + Pfizer jointly fund retraining for affected workers into new roles (clinical research, data analysis, healthcare tech)
- **Business case:** Pfizer saves on turnover costs; DCRI gains trained workforce; workers don't become unemployed
- **Graph support:** #6 (turnover ROI), #7 (retention), #8 (profitability)

#### Model 2: Wage-Sharing from Automation Gains
- **Setup:** Pfizer uses AI to automate manufacturing task → productivity gains shared (e.g., 30% gain = 10% goes back to workers whose roles were automated)
- **Business case:** Pfizer still gets 70% benefit; workers aren't left behind; income stays in community
- **Graph support:** #2 (wage vs. automation), #8 (profitability), #10 (wage-sharing models)

#### Model 3: Job Ladder Preservation
- **Setup:** Don't eliminate entry-level roles when automating mid-level ones. Automate mid-level, promote from entry, hire new entry-level workers
- **Business case:** Maintains talent pipeline; keeps community healthy; easier recruiting
- **Graph support:** #5 (community spending), #7 (retention), #9 (community health)

#### Model 4: Transition Fund
- **Setup:** For every role automated, Pfizer + DCRI allocate funds to support 2-3 years of retraining/living stipend for affected workers
- **Business case:** Cost < turnover + community decline costs; workers stay in market; community stable
- **Graph support:** #3 (cost of living), #6 (turnover ROI), #8 (profitability)
