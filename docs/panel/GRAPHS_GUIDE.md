# Duke Tech Expo 2026 — Panel Discussion Graphs Guide
## "AI, Economic Inequality, and the Future of Work"

**Purpose:** Economic data visualization recommendations for panel discussion on AI's distributional impact. This guide curates 18 graphs ranked by impact and analytical value, highlighting which metrics reveal inequality vs. which mask it.

**Key Principle:** GDP and aggregate averages hide inequality. Distributional metrics (wage gaps, income concentration, job polarization) reveal the true story.

---

## 🔴 TOP PICK — Most Impactful Opening Graph

### 1. **CEO-to-Worker Pay Ratio: 1965 vs 2025 (Trend Line)**
- **Why lead with this:** Visceral, long-standing, directly shows reward concentration divergence
- **Visual:** Shows ratio rising from **21:1 (1965)** → **341:1 (2025)** — a 16x increase in 60 years
- **What it reveals:** Not an AI issue (existed since 1980s) but foundational context for why AI's distributional impact matters
- **What it hides:** Productivity gains of workers (they've contributed, but not rewarded equally)
- **Source:** EPI CEO Pay Report 2025; IPS Executive Excess Report 2025
- **Links:** https://www.epi.org/publication/ceo-pay/ | https://ips-dc.org/report-executive-excess-2025/

## PART I: Foundational Inequality (Pre-AI, Long-Standing)

### 2. **Wage Growth vs. Productivity Growth: 1979–2025 (Dual Trend)**
- **Why it matters:** Root cause of wage stagnation — workers produce more, earn less
- **Visual:** Productivity +90%, worker compensation +33% — lines diverging sharply after 1979
- **What it reveals:** Decoupling from policy shifts (union decline), not technology alone
- **Source:** EPI, BLS | **Links:** https://www.epi.org/productivity-pay-gap/

### 3. **Gini Coefficient Trend: United States 1970–2025**
- **Why it matters:** Standard measure of income inequality, internationally comparable
- **Visual:** Rising 0.39 (1970s) → 0.48 (2024)
- **What it reveals:** U.S. among highest inequality of developed nations
- **Source:** Bureau of Economic Analysis, World Bank | **Links:** https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.GINI

### 4. **Income Share of Top 1%, Top 10%, Bottom 50%: 1980–2025**
- **Why it matters:** Shows concentration, not just spread
- **Visual:** Top 1% rising 10% → 23.6%; Bottom 50% ~20% → ~8%
- **What it reveals:** Massive income shift to top, especially post-2008
- **Source:** World Inequality Database | **Links:** https://wid.world/

### 5. **Wealth Held by Top 1%: Q1 2024 Snapshot**
- **Why it matters:** Wealth = power (stocks, investment income, legacy, political influence)
- **Visual:** Top 1% owns 30.5% of all wealth; Bottom 50% owns 2.5%
- **What it reveals:** Wealth concentration 2–3x worse than income concentration
- **Source:** Federal Reserve Board, Census Bureau | **Links:** https://inequality.org/article/billionaire-wealth-concentration-is-even-worse-than-you-imagine/

## PART II: The Misleading Graphs (GDP & Aggregates)

### 6. **GDP Per Capita vs. Median Wage Growth: 1979–2025**
- **Why it matters:** The "GDP illusion" — why growth stats can lie to workers
- **Visual:** GDP per capita +100%; median wage +33% (same data, different stories)
- **What it reveals:** Growth captured by top, not distributed broadly
- **Why misleading:** GDP = average (skewed by billionaires); median = typical experience
- **Source:** BLS, FRED | **Links:** https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GDPC1

### 7. **Stock Market Returns vs. Wages: 1989–2025**
- **Why it matters:** Shows where capital gains concentrate
- **Visual:** S&P 500 up ~600%; real median wage up ~30%
- **What it reveals:** Stock ownership (top 10% own 87%, bottom 50% own 1%) = where growth goes
- **Source:** Federal Reserve, S&P Dow Jones | **Links:** https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500

## PART III: AI-Specific Labor Market Impacts

### 8. **AI Wage Gap: 2026 (AI Skills Premium)**
- **Why it matters:** Shows AI creating wage bifurcation NOW
- **Visual:** AI-skilled workers earning 23–56% higher salary than peers (Q1 2026)
- **What it reveals:** Skills premium is real; most workers lack AI skills; retraining access unequal
- **Source:** LinkedIn, IDC, Anthropic | **Links:** https://aiwagegap.com/

### 9. **Job Exposure to AI by Occupational Wage (U-Shape)**
- **Why it matters:** Shows which jobs at risk — not what people think
- **Visual:** Low-wage service insulated (non-routine manual); middle-wage routine exposed (admin, coding); high-wage augmented (safe/grow)
- **What it reveals:** Hollowing of middle continues; low-wage workers insulated but not better off
- **Source:** Anthropic, BLS, Yale Budget Lab | **Links:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts

### 10. **Occupational Wage Polarization: 1980–2020**
- **Why it matters:** Shows labor market transformation shape (not new, AI will accelerate)
- **Visual:** U-shape: high-wage grow, low-wage grow, middle-wage shrink
- **What it reveals:** Automation targets routine tasks; non-routine (both types) survives
- **What it hides:** This is reversible with policy (union strength, wage floors, etc.)
- **Source:** MIT Autor, Federal Reserve, NBER | **Links:** https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/The%20Polarization%20of%20the%20U.S.%20Labor%20Market%20-%20NBER.pdf

### 11. **AI Job Displacement vs. Creation Forecast: 2026–2030**
- **Why it matters:** Quantifies "net churn" — not zero-sum, but unequal
- **Visual:** 83M jobs displaced globally by 2027; 69M created → 23% net churn (WEF)
- **What it reveals:** Disruption is real, but aggregate job count might not crater
- **What it hides:** "New jobs" often lower-wage or require retraining; displaced workers don't auto-fill
- **Source:** WEF, Goldman Sachs | **Links:** https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/02/ai-jobs-future-of-work/

### 12. **Productivity Gains from AI vs. Wage Growth Forecast: 2025–2030**
- **Why it matters:** Key question: Do productivity gains translate to wage gains?
- **Visual:** AI productivity up 15–25% projected; wage growth 1–3% (flat to inflation)
- **What it reveals:** Past 40 years of wage decoupling suggest AI gains won't flow to workers *unless we make it so*
- **What it hides:** This outcome is NOT inevitable — policy choices matter
- **Source:** McKinsey, BCG, Microsoft, Federal Reserve

### 13. **Hiring Trends for Young Workers in AI-Exposed Occupations: 2020–2026**
- **Why it matters:** Real-time impact on entry-level workers (future earners)
- **Visual:** Hiring rate for 22–25 year-olds in routine cognitive jobs declining since late 2022
- **What it reveals:** Younger workers already squeezed out of middle-wage entry points
- **Source:** Anthropic, BLS, LinkedIn | **Links:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts

## PART IV: Comparative Metrics (What Reveals vs. Hides)

### 14. **Real Wage vs. Nominal Wage Growth: 1979–2025**
- **Why it matters:** Is wage "growth" real or just inflation?
- **Visual:** Nominal up 150%; real (inflation-adjusted) up 33%
- **What it reveals:** Inflation has eaten most nominal gains, especially healthcare/housing/education
- **Source:** BLS, FRED | **Links:** https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CEENUUS

### 15. **Income Growth by Percentile: 1980–2020**
- **Why it matters:** Shows whose wages grew (answer: the top)
- **Visual:** 90th percentile +50%; 50th +20%; 10th -5%
- **What it reveals:** Growth NOT equally distributed; bottom being left behind
- **Source:** EPI, Pew Research | **Links:** https://www.epi.org/publication/charting-wage-stagnation/

### 16. **Intergenerational Income Mobility: U.S. vs. Peer Nations**
- **Why it matters:** Can AI/automation trap people in poverty or enable escape?
- **Visual:** U.S. IGE ~0.6 (60% of parents' income determines kids'); Scandinavia ~0.15 (15%)
- **What it reveals:** U.S. offers less opportunity than peers; policy failure, not market
- **What it hides:** This can be changed (Scandinavia proves it)
- **Source:** Federal Reserve Bank Chicago, OECD | **Links:** https://www.chicagofed.org/research/content-areas/mobility/intergenerational-economic-mobility

### 17. **Skills Gap in AI: % of Workforce Needing Reskilling by 2030**
- **Why it matters:** Scale of retraining challenge (is it realistic?)
- **Visual:** WEF: 59% of global workforce needs reskilling; IDC: 90% of enterprises will face critical skills shortages
- **What it reveals:** Retraining is stated solution, but scale is massive
- **What it hides:** Cost, time, access inequality
- **Source:** WEF, IDC, Deloitte | **Links:** https://www.weforum.org/stories/2024/01/world-economic-forum-future-of-jobs-report-2024/

### 18. **Wealth Inequality by Race & Ethnicity: 2024**
- **Why it matters:** AI risks widening existing racial inequalities
- **Visual:** White families ~$440K median wealth; Black ~$160K; Hispanic ~$170K
- **What it reveals:** 2–3x wealth gaps; these determine who can retrain, who owns AI cos, who captures gains
- **What it hides:** These gaps are CAUSED (redlining, wage discrimination) — history matters
- **Source:** Federal Reserve SCF, Census, Urban Institute | **Links:** https://apps.urban.org/features/wealth-inequality-charts/

## Recommended Panel Sequence (3–4 Graphs)

**Pick 3:**
1. **#1: CEO-to-Worker Ratio** — visceral opening
2. **#6: GDP vs. Median Wage** — expose the economy strength lie
3. **#8 or #11: AI Wage Gap or Job Displacement** — connect to AI

**Add 4th:**
4. **#4 or #18: Income Share / Racial Wealth Gap** — who benefits, who's left behind

**Talking Points by Theme:**
- "Inequality is long-standing" → use 2, 3, 4, 14
- "Economy's 'strong' but workers struggle" → use 6, 7
- "AI makes it worse" → use 8, 9, 11, 12
- "This is fixable, not destiny" → use 15, 16, 17, 18
