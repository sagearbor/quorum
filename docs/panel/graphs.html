Topic: "AI, Economic Inequality, and the Future of Work"

1. CEO-to-Worker Pay Ratio: 1965 vs 2025
🔴 TOP PICK — Most Impactful Opening
The Story: CEO pay rose from 21:1 (1965) → 341:1 (2025). A 16x increase in 60 years.

Why Lead With This: Visceral, long-standing, directly shows how reward concentration has diverged from worker productivity.

💬 Talking Point:
"Over 60 years, CEO pay grew 1,094% while typical worker pay grew 26%. This ratio — not a new problem, but AI will likely accelerate it unless we're intentional about distribution."

What It Reveals: Not an AI issue (existed since 1980s) but foundational context for why AI's distributional impact matters.

What It Hides: Productivity gains of workers (they've contributed, but aren't rewarded equally).

Sources: Economic Policy Institute (EPI), Institute for Policy Studies (IPS)

2. GDP Per Capita vs. Median Wage Growth: 1979–2025
Why "Economy is Strong" is a Lie
The Gap: GDP per capita up ~100% | Median wage up ~33%

Why It Matters: This is the classic "GDP illusion" — why growth statistics can lie to workers.

💬 Talking Point:
"If your policy makers cite GDP growth to justify the status quo, they're using a metric that masks your reality. Median wage is what you actually experienced."

What It Reveals: Growth has been captured by the top, not distributed broadly.

What It Hides: GDP = average (skewed by billionaires); median = typical experience.

Sources: BLS, Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED)

View FRED GDP Data
3. AI Wage Gap: 2026 (AI Skills Premium)
The Inequality Happening RIGHT NOW
The Premium: AI-skilled workers earning 23–56% higher salary than peers (Q1 2026)

Why It Matters: Shows how AI is creating wage bifurcation NOW, before AI reaches peak productivity.

💬 Talking Point:
"AI-skilled workers earn 23–56% more than peers. This is happening now, before AI reaches peak productivity. The wage gap is your future unless upskilling is equitable."

What It Reveals: Skills premium is real; most workers lack AI skills; retraining access is unequal.

What It Hides: That most workers don't have AI skills and retraining takes time/money (access inequality).

Sources: LinkedIn Salary Data, IDC, Anthropic

View AI Wage Gap Data
4. Income Share of Top 1%, Top 10%, Bottom 50%: 1980–2025
Where the Money Really Goes
The Shift: Top 1% from 10% → 23.6% | Bottom 50% from 20% → 8%

Why It Matters: Shows concentration, not just spread. Where does the money go?

💬 Talking Point:
"Top 1% now captures 23.6¢ of every dollar of new income. Bottom 50% gets 8¢. This is the inequality structure AI doesn't solve — it typically worsens."

What It Reveals: Massive income shift to the top, especially post-2008 financial crisis.

What It Hides: Heterogeneity within groups (not all top 1% are the same; not all bottom 50% are the same).

Sources: World Inequality Database (WID), Piketty et al., Our World in Data

5. Wealth Held by Top 1%: Q1 2024 (Racial Breakdown)
Who Owns the Power
The Snapshot:
• Top 1% owns 30.5% of all wealth | Bottom 50% owns 2.5%
• White families: ~$440K median wealth
• Black families: ~$160K | Hispanic families: ~$170K

Why It Matters: Wealth = power (stocks, investment income, legacy, political influence). And it's racialized.

💬 Talking Point:
"Black families own 1/3 the wealth of White families (on average). If AI capital gains flow to existing wealth, this gap gets 3x worse by 2030. Intentional policy is required to break the cycle."

What It Reveals: Extreme wealth concentration (2–3x worse than income concentration) and deep racial inequalities.

What It Hides: That these gaps are CAUSED, not natural (redlining, wage discrimination, etc. — history matters).

Sources: Federal Reserve Board, Census Bureau, Urban Institute
